# Editing Rules — Story First

These rules are mandatory for short-form anime/video edits in runner-4.

## Source selection
- Use one continuous scene or one continuous fight as the primary source.
- Do not assemble a story from unrelated promo shots, trailers, game footage, or multiple fights simply because the frames look exciting.
- Prefer a source window long enough to contain setup -> escalation -> reversal -> payoff.

## Story gate before render
Before beat mapping, write a 4-6 line micro-story for the selected source window:
1. Who is acting?
2. What does that character want?
3. What changes or goes wrong?
4. What is the strongest reversal/impact?
5. What is the payoff?

If the source cannot answer these coherently, reject the source before editing.

## Timeline rules
- Default to chronological ordering.
- At most one future hook at the beginning and one callback at the end for looping.
- No arbitrary time jumps just to hit beats.
- Every cut must preserve action continuity, causality, or a deliberate reaction shot.

## Beat rules
- Music supports the story; music does not decide shot order.
- Hard beats may trigger impact cuts, speed ramps, flashes, shakes, or reframes.
- Minor beats should usually stay inside the same shot via motion/velocity rather than force a cut.
- Fewer coherent shots are preferred over many disconnected cuts.

## Framing
- 1080x1920 master.
- Protect an iPhone-safe central viewport of about 886 px width.
- Keep faces, hands/weapons, opponent, and the action axis inside the safe region when they matter.
- Avoid destructive landscape-to-9:16 crops when the narrative information sits outside the center.

## QA gates
A render is not final until all gates pass:
1. Story contact sheet is understandable without audio.
2. Master 9:16 contact sheet has no logos/credits/UI mistakes unless intentionally retained.
3. iPhone 19.5:9 center-cover simulation preserves the critical subject/action.
4. No repeated filler shot unless it is an intentional callback/loop.
5. A viewer can answer: who fought whom, what changed, and what the payoff was.

## Failure rule
If the edit feels like "random cool shots on beat", discard the edit plan instead of adding more effects.
